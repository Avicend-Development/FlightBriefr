Lesson Objectives

  • Perform coordinated 360° turns at exactly 45° angle of bank — both left and right
  • Maintain altitude, heading accuracy and coordination throughout the full 360°

  • Recognise and manage overbanking tendency with light opposite aileron pressure

Motivation

Steep turns are the foundation of advanced airwork: 720° turns, spiral recoveries, and evasive manoeuvring. Proficiency at 45° bank demands smooth, coordinated control pressure and accurate attitude flying — skills that transfer directly to all advanced flight phases. Mastering the forces at 45° bank also builds a deep, intuitive understanding of load factor and stall-speed increase — essential safety knowledge for every PPL pilot.

Common Mistakes

  • Applying aileron first, then waiting before adding back-pressure — causes a slip entry and altitude loss
  • Not adding power on entry — speed decays, altitude lost
  • Left turn: not adding power immediately (Pitot on left wing causes early IAS indication drop)
  • Forgetting to clear the area and complete ACSEL before initiating the turn
  • Not starting roll-out 10°–20° before target heading — overshooting
  • Failing to reduce power on roll-out — aircraft climbs as wings level
  • Over-correcting back-pressure → increased load factor → approaching accelerated stall
  • Attempting to raise a low nose by back-pressure alone — must reduce bank first

ENVIRONMENT

FORCES IN A STEEP TURN · LOAD FACTOR · AIRMANSHIP

Theory — Forces in a Steep Turn

In a turn, lift must overcome both weight and centrifugal force. As bank increases, the vertical component of lift decreases — requiring increased back-pressure to maintain altitude. At 45° bank the load factor is approximately 1.41 g, raising the stall speed by ~19%. Drag increases significantly, so power must be added to maintain airspeed.

The relationship is: Load Factor = 1 / cos(bank angle). At 45°: LF = 1/cos(45°) = 1.41 g. Stall speed increase = Vs × √LF = Vs × 1.19.

Theory — Overbanking Tendency

At 45° and above, the outside wing travels faster than the inside wing, generating more lift on the outside. This causes the bank to steepen automatically unless checked with opposite (top) aileron pressure. This overbanking tendency distinguishes steep turns from medium turns where inherent stability holds the bank.

Theory — Load Factor & Accelerated Stall

Excessive back-pressure in a steep turn tightens the turn radius, further increases load factor, and can lead to an accelerated stall — at a higher speed than the published Vs. Warning signs: buffet onset, reduced control effectiveness, high sink rate. Recovery: reduce bank first, then raise nose — never attempt to recover with elevator alone from 45°+.

Training Conditions (ATP INT. DGH 03)

  • Good visibility and cloudbase — VMC throughout
  • Wind: non-gusty, ideally ≤15 kt for initial introduction
  • Suggested area: W / SW of Roskilde CTR (EKRK), 1,000–2,500 ft
  • Clear of controlled airspace, populated areas, and cloud
  • Minimum altitude for steep turns: per ACSEL — sufficient height for recovery if accelerated stall occurs

Airmanship — Collision Avoidance (EGAST GA1)

  • Collisions are most likely when aircraft are turning — heightened awareness throughout this exercise
  • Perform clearing turns before any manoeuvre: look, then turn
  • During 360° turns: ~85% time on outside lookout, 10% attitude check, 5% instruments
  • High-wing aircraft: lift wing slightly before turning to clear the blind spot below
  • ACSEL check: do not proceed without completing it aloud

02

SETUP

AIRCRAFT CONFIGURATION · ACSEL CHECK · VISUAL REFERENCE SELECTION

Theory — Entry Preparation

Before every steep turn, the area must be cleared with a deliberate clearing turn. The pilot selects a fixed visual reference on the horizon (or sets the HDG bug) to mark the entry and roll-out heading — this prevents spatial disorientation during the 360° and anchors awareness of when roll-out should begin (10°–20° lead).

Trim the aircraft for level cruise. Verify engine instruments are in the green. Note the PA28's inherent lateral stability: above 30° bank, overbanking tendency replaces the natural tendency to return to wings level — the aircraft will steepen unless actively checked.

ACSEL Check (CAPA SOP)

Call out and complete before entering the manoeuvre:

  • A — Altitude: "…ft. Manoeuvre above [minimum] ft AGL."
  • C — Configuration: "Steep turn, clean, cruise speed."
  • S — Safety: "Loose objects stowed, harness fastened."
  • E — Engine: "Instruments in the green, set for airwork."
  • L — Location: "Clear of airspace, populated areas, cloud — clearing turns complete."

Call out: "ACSEL check completed."

Aircraft Configuration

  • Speed: normal cruise (~100 KIAS) — do not exceed VA
  • Configuration: clean — flaps up
  • Power: set cruise MAP/RPM
  • Trim: aircraft in trim for straight and level flight
  • Carburettor heat: as required per conditions / POH

Visual Reference Selection

  • Choose a prominent geographic feature or heading on the horizon to mark the entry/exit heading
  • Set HDG bug to current heading before entry
  • Side-by-side seating note: in a left turn, the nose appears to rise; in a right turn, it appears to lower — compensate consciously for this sight picture difference
  • Roll-out lead: begin 10°–20° before the target heading / visual feature

03

EXECUTION

ENTRY · IN TURN · ROLL-OUT · INSTRUCTOR TECHNIQUE

Key principle (CAPA SOP §1.22.4): Control pressure, not control movement. Aileron and back-pressure must be applied simultaneously as bank increases — never wait for the bank to establish before adding elevator input. Think attitude; think coordinated pressure — not "step" inputs.

ENTRY — 0° TO 45° BANK

  1. Clear the area: look in the direction of turn before rolling. ACSEL complete.
  2. Apply aileron in the direction of turn with coordinated rudder.
  3. As bank passes 30°: simultaneously add back-pressure AND increase power to maintain speed and altitude.
    Left turn only: start adding power immediately with the roll — Pitot on the left wing causes early IAS indication drop.
  4. At 45° bank: neutralise aileron and rudder.
  5. Hold back-pressure to keep nose at the level-turn attitude on the horizon. Cross-check: altimeter → nose position → wing angle.

IN THE TURN — MAINTAINING 45°

  1. Aileron: hold light opposite (top) aileron to counter overbanking tendency.
  2. Elevator: maintain constant back-pressure — nose at horizon level-turn attitude.
  3. Rudder: use for coordination — keep the ball centred.
  4. Power: maintain as set to sustain cruise speed — watch for speed decay.
  5. If nose drops too far below horizon: reduce bank first, then raise nose. Never use elevator alone to recover a low nose at 45°+.
  6. Scan: attitude → altimeter → AI → ball. 85% focus outside on visual references.

ROLL-OUT — 45° TO WINGS LEVEL

  1. Begin roll-out 10°–20° before the target heading / visual reference.
  2. Apply opposite aileron and coordinated rudder to roll wings level.
  3. Simultaneously ease off back-pressure as bank decreases.
  4. Reduce power as bank passes through 30° on roll-out.
    Left turn: reduce power simultaneously with roll-out start.
  5. As wings level: neutralise all control inputs. Re-trim for straight and level.
  6. Cross-check altimeter. Call out heading achieved.

Instructor Technique Notes

  • Demo first with "talk-through" narration; student flies from the second attempt
  • Introduce right turn first — more natural sight picture for left-seat pilot; introduce left turn second (note Pitot effect)
  • If time permits, progress to 720° turns once 360° is consistent at standard
  • Cover AI/DG on later practice attempts to reinforce visual attitude flying
  • Overbanking tendency: only correct this verbally if it becomes a habitual problem — early coaching causes over-correction
  • Typical altitude error: diving entry then climbing roll-out — brief simultaneous aileron and back-pressure application

04

CHECK

ASSESSMENT · DEBRIEF · STANDARDS · PROGRESSION

Lesson Standard — Pass / Progress Criteria

The student demonstrates 360° turns at 45° bank, both left and right, meeting all four tolerances simultaneously (±100 ft altitude, ±5° bank, ±10° roll-out heading, ±5 kt airspeed) for a minimum of one complete revolution in each direction. Coordination is maintained (ball centred) throughout entry, turn and roll-out. ACSEL check is completed before each manoeuvre. Lookout is continuous.

Lookout & ACSEL

Clearing turns completed. ACSEL check called out correctly in full. Area confirmed clear before every entry.

Entry Coordination

Aileron and back-pressure applied simultaneously as bank increases. No slip/skid on entry. Power added correctly — immediately in left turn.

Bank Accuracy

45° ±5° maintained throughout. Overbanking managed with light opposite aileron. No excessive correction inputs.

Altitude Control

Altitude held within ±100 ft. No significant altitude gain on roll-out. Nose attitude correctly referenced to horizon.

Coordination (Ball)

Ball centred throughout entry, turn and roll-out. Rudder used for balance — not for direction. No skids or slips.

Roll-Out Accuracy

Roll-out commenced 10°–20° before target. Wings level within ±10° of entry heading. Back-pressure released progressively. Power reduced on roll-out.

Debrief Points — Typical Issues

  • Slip on entry: delayed back-pressure — brief "simultaneous aileron and elevator pressure"
  • Altitude loss: insufficient power or back-pressure — review forces in turn, load factor diagram
  • Nose too low: student attempts elevator alone — reinforce "reduce bank first, then raise nose"
  • Heading overshoot: late roll-out — practise half-bank lead rule (10°–20° before target)
  • Speed decay in left turn: Pitot effect — must add power immediately on left roll, before speed decays
  • Climbing roll-out: failed to reduce power — emphasise power reduction as bank decreases through 30°

Progression & Next Lesson

  • If 360° achieved at standard → progress to 720° turns
  • Link to stall awareness: demonstrate accelerated stall entry from steep turn to reinforce load factor (ACSEL required, EGAST GA8)
  • Reinforce collision avoidance: turning flight is highest-risk phase for mid-air collision — lookout habit is non-negotiable
  • Future exercises building on steep turns: steep spirals, unusual attitude recovery, emergency turns
