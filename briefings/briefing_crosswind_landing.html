Lesson Objectives

  • Calculate the crosswind component from ATIS and assess against PA28 limits before flight
  • Explain and distinguish between the crab, wing-low, and combination techniques
  • Perform a stabilised crosswind approach to threshold, maintaining centreline and glidepath
  • Transition correctly to wing-low (sideslip) technique at 300 ft AGL
  • Execute a touchdown with the upwind wheel first, aligned with the runway heading, on centreline
  • Apply continuous into-wind aileron and rudder control during the landing roll-out

Motivation

Crosswind landings account for a disproportionately high share of GA runway-excursion and ground-loop accidents. The PA28 has a max demonstrated crosswind of 17 kt, but technique failures cause accidents well below this value. Mastery of the wing-low technique — using aileron and rudder independently — builds the coordination foundation for all asymmetric and unusual attitude recovery work. This lesson directly protects the student's future as a safe, solo pilot.

Common Mistakes

  • Using aileron as if for a turn (banking hard) instead of maintaining a controlled sideslip with opposite rudder
  • Releasing the into-wind aileron too early in the flare — aircraft drifts at touchdown
  • Touching down in a drift — lateral load on undercarriage; potential for ground loop
  • Forgetting the final check during the approach workload (ATP INT. DGH 06)
  • Late or no go-around when approach is clearly not stabilised — hesitation, then committing to a bad approach
  • Forgetting to set carburettor heat to COLD during go-around (ATP INT. DGH 06)
  • Not maintaining into-wind aileron after touchdown — relaxing control during ground roll
  • Correcting drift on short final with rudder alone instead of rebalancing sideslip

ENVIRONMENT

CROSSWIND THEORY · DRIFT & SIDESLIP · AIRMANSHIP

Theory — Drift and Crab Angle

When flying a heading aligned with the runway centreline in a crosswind, the aircraft drifts downwind. To track the extended centreline on final approach, a crab angle must be flown — the nose is angled into wind so the ground track equals the runway track. The crab angle depends on the crosswind component and the aircraft's groundspeed: stronger crosswind or slower approach speed = larger crab angle.

Crab is comfortable and efficient during the approach, but if maintained to touchdown, the aircraft will track correctly but land with a yaw angle relative to the runway, causing side-loads on the undercarriage.

Theory — Sideslip (Wing-Low Technique)

In a sideslip, the aircraft is deliberately slipped into wind: into-wind (upwind) aileron lowers the upwind wing, and downwind (opposite) rudder keeps the nose aligned with the runway heading. The aircraft tracks the centreline while the fuselage acts as a skid. The upwind main gear touches first, absorbing lateral force progressively. This is the safest technique for landing in a crosswind.

Note: in a sideslip the balance ball is displaced toward the into-wind side. This is correct and intentional — brief the student that a centred ball in this phase indicates the sideslip has been lost.

Crosswind Component (ATP INT. DGH 06)

  • XWC = Wind speed × sin(angle from runway heading)
  • Example: 15 kt at 30° off runway = 7.5 kt crosswind
  • PA28-161 max demonstrated crosswind: 17 kt
  • CAPA limit for student solo: check current SOPs / FI guidance
  • Always calculate from ATIS before flight. If at or near limit: reassess after landing inspection

Training Conditions (ATP INT. DGH 06)

  • Good visibility and cloudbase — VMC, EKRK circuit
  • Crosswind: introduce at 5–10 kt component; build toward limit progressively
  • Non-gusty conditions preferred for initial introduction
  • PAPI serviceable — approach below 2° angle (4× red) requires go-around

SETUP

ATIS · CROSSWIND CALC · APPROACH CONFIG · THREE TECHNIQUES

Pre-Flight — ATIS Assessment

  • Obtain ATIS: note wind direction, speed, gusts, active runway
  • Calculate crosswind component — if close to limit, re-evaluate inflight
  • Note gust factor: if gusts ≥10 kt, add half the gust increment to Vapp: e.g. wind 10G20 kt → add 5 kt to Vapp = 80 KIAS
  • Consider flap setting: in strong crosswind, flaps 25° (not full) preserves aileron authority and reduces weathercocking tendency

The Three Techniques

  • Crab method: maintain crab throughout approach, straighten to runway heading at the last moment before touchdown. Risk: mistiming the straightening — lateral drift at touchdown.
  • Wing-low (sideslip): maintain runway heading throughout using into-wind aileron + opposite rudder. Most reliable for touchdown precision.
  • Combination (CAPA recommended): crab from base to 300 ft AGL, then transition to wing-low for the last 300 ft and landing. Best of both: accurate tracking on approach, precise touchdown.

Aircraft Configuration — Crosswind Landing

  • Flaps: 25° (preferred in crosswind ≥10 kt) — full flap in strong crosswind reduces aileron authority
  • Approach speed (Vapp): 75 KIAS clean; adjust +5 kt for gusts
  • Trim: trimmed for approach speed — allows precise control pressure
  • Carb heat: as required for conditions / per normal approach procedure

Stabilised Approach Criteria (CAPA SOP §3.4)

By 300 ft AGL the approach must be stabilised: correct speed, on glidepath (2 white / 2 red PAPI), on centreline, correct configuration. If any criterion is not met by 300 ft: go-around.

Call-out at 300 ft: "STABLE" (or initiate go-around).

EXECUTION

FINAL APPROACH · TRANSITION TO WING-LOW · FLARE & TOUCHDOWN · GO-AROUND

Key principle (CAPA SOP §3.5): Forget about the flight controls — fly the aircraft. The goal is to maintain the correct runway alignment and centreline tracking. Aileron controls lateral position; rudder controls heading. They are used independently in the sideslip, not as a coordinated pair. Keep the upwind wing down and the nose on the runway heading until the wheels are on the ground.

FINAL APPROACH — CRAB PHASE

  1. Turn onto final. Assess drift: if drifting, increase crab angle toward wind until centreline is maintained.
  2. Set approach configuration: flaps 25°, 75 KIAS. Trim for approach speed.
  3. PAPI check: aim for 2 white / 2 red. Power controls glidepath; attitude controls airspeed.
  4. Complete final check (CAPA SOP): call out all items.
  5. At 300 ft: assess — stabilised? Call "STABLE" or initiate go-around immediately.
  6. Maintain crab angle to 300 ft, tracking centreline precisely with visual reference to runway.

TRANSITION TO WING-LOW (300 ft AGL)

  1. At 300 ft AGL: smoothly apply into-wind aileron — lower the upwind wing 5–10°.
  2. Simultaneously apply opposite (downwind) rudder to maintain runway heading — prevent the aircraft from turning into the bank.
  3. Adjust aileron to maintain centreline — more aileron if drifting downwind, less if tracking accurately.
  4. Adjust rudder continuously to hold runway heading — the balance ball will be displaced into-wind. This is correct.
  5. Continue approach: attitude controls airspeed, power controls glidepath.

FLARE, TOUCHDOWN & GROUND ROLL

  1. Flare normally: reduce power progressively, raise nose gently to arrest rate of descent.
  2. Maintain into-wind aileron and downwind rudder throughout the flare — do not relax the sideslip.
  3. Touchdown: upwind main wheel first, then downwind main, then nose wheel. Call "UPWIND WHEEL."
  4. At and after touchdown: increase into-wind aileron progressively as speed reduces (less aerodynamic effect at slower speed).
  5. Ground roll: maintain directional control with rudder / nosewheel. Braking: even application.
  6. Vacate runway clear, then complete after-landing checks.

Go-Around — Decision Points

  • Not stabilised by 300 ft AGL → immediate go-around. Do not attempt to "save" the approach.
  • Drift at flare → go-around
  • Not aligned with runway at touchdown height → go-around
  • Go-around: full power, carb heat COLD, positive climb attitude, flaps to 10° (then retract progressively above Vfe), call ATC

Instructor Technique Notes

  • Demonstrate first circuit with commentary on crab angle and sideslip inputs
  • Student takes control from base turn on second circuit — FI monitors approach from 500 ft
  • Cover the ASI briefly on later circuits to reinforce attitude flying on final
  • Typical intervention point: student releases sideslip in the flare — coach "hold the bank"
  • Progress: introduce gusts once basic technique is consistent — brief gust-correction technique

CHECK

ASSESSMENT · DEBRIEF · STANDARDS · PROGRESSION

Lesson Standard — Pass / Progress Criteria

The student correctly calculates the crosswind component, performs a stabilised final approach at 75 KIAS on centreline and glidepath, transitions to the wing-low technique at 300 ft, maintains runway heading and centreline throughout the flare, achieves touchdown on the upwind wheel with no drift, and applies into-wind aileron during the ground roll. Go-around is initiated promptly and correctly when the approach is not stabilised. Final check is completed on every approach.

ATIS & XWC Calc

Crosswind component calculated from ATIS. Within PA28 limits confirmed. Gust additive applied to Vapp if required. Flap setting appropriate for conditions.

Stabilised Approach

"STABLE" called or go-around initiated at 300 ft. Speed within +10/–0 kt of Vapp. PAPI: no more than 3 red. Centreline maintained.

Final Check

Final check completed and called out correctly on every approach. No items omitted under workload. Carb heat actioned correctly.

Wing-Low Technique

Into-wind aileron applied at 300 ft. Opposite rudder maintains runway heading. Ball displaced into-wind (correct). Sideslip maintained to touchdown without releasing.

Touchdown Quality

Upwind wheel first. No lateral drift at touchdown. On centreline. Runway heading maintained at touchdown. Into-wind aileron increased on roll-out.

Go-Around Decision

Go-around initiated promptly and without hesitation when approach is not stabilised. Full procedure correct: power, attitude, config, carb heat COLD, ATC call.

Debrief Points — Typical Issues

  • Drift at touchdown: sideslip released too early in flare — brief "hold the bank until the wheels are on"
  • Ball centred in sideslip: student coordinating instead of slipping — re-brief independent use of aileron and rudder
  • Late go-around: student committed to bad approach — re-emphasise 300 ft decision gate, no exceptions
  • Carb heat left ON during go-around (ATP INT. DGH 06): brief the full go-around flow immediately
  • Wrong direction sideslip: student slipped downwind — re-brief "aileron INTO the wind, upwind wing down"

Progression & Next Lesson

  • If technique consistent → solo crosswind circuits (check CAPA XWC limit for solo)
  • Progress to gusty crosswind conditions — brief gust-additive technique
  • Link to short-field crosswind landing once standard approach is mastered
  • Reinforce go-around habit: every lesson, every approach — the decision is always available
